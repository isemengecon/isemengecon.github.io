Breakeven Analysis

A mining company is considering whether to invest in a newly discovered mine. The mine is expected to yield 100 kg of gold annually over the next five years. It costs $17 million to purchase the mine. Furthermore, the operating cost in the first year is $1 million and is estimated to increase 10% each year. The price of the newly extracted gold is currently $50,000 per kg and is estimated to increase 5% annually (i.e., the price of the newly extracted gold in the first year of operations is estimated to be $52,500 per kg). Assume that the company uses an annual interest of 7% in their decision making.

Q1. After one year of operations, the cash flow of this investment is:

• A0 = -$17,000,000

• A1 = 100 x $52,500 – $1,000,000 = $4,250,000

Based on the cash flow described above, the present value of this investment after one year is $.

          

-$17M + $4.25M ÷ 1.07 = -$13,028,037




Q2. The operating cost in the second year is estimated to be $.

          

$1,000,000 x 1.1 = $1,100,000




Q3. The price of newly extracted gold in the second year of operations is estimated to be $ per kg.

          

$50,000 x 1.052 = $55,125




Q4. Based on your solutions from Questions 1 to 3, the present value of this investment after two years is $.

          

-$13,028,037 + (100 x $55,125 – $1,100,000) ÷ 1.072 = -$9,173,989




Q5. The operating cost in the third year is estimated to be $.

          

$1,100,000 x 1.1 = $1,210,000




Q6. The price of newly extracted gold in the third year of operations is estimated to be $ per kg.

          

$50,000 x 1.053 = $57,881




Q7. Based on your solutions from Questions 4 to 6, the present value of this investment after three years is $.

          

-$9,173,989 + (100 x $57,881 – $1,210,000) ÷ 1.073 = -$5,436,875




Q8. The operating cost in the fourth year is estimated to be $.

          

$1,210,000 x 1.1 = $1,331,000




Q9. The price of newly extracted gold in the fourth year of operations is estimated to be $ per kg.

          

$50,000 x 1.054 = $60,775




Q10. Based on your solutions from Questions 7 to 9, the present value of this investment after four years is $.

          

-$5,436,875 + (100 x $60,775 – $1,331,000) ÷ 1.074 = -$1,815,769




Q11. The operating cost in the fifth year is estimated to be $.

          

$1,331,000 x 1.1 = $1,464,100




Q12. The price of newly extracted gold in the fifth year of operations is estimated to be $ per kg.

          

$50,000 x 1.055 = $63,814




Q13. Based on your solutions from Questions 10 to 12, the present value of this investment after five years is $.

Risk Analysis

A mining company is considering whether to invest in a newly discovered mine. The mine is expected to yield 100 kg of gold annually. There is a 0.25 probability the mine contains 300 kg of gold (i.e., last three years), 0.5 probability it contains 500 kg of gold (i.e., last five years) and 0.25 probability it contains 600 kg of gold (i.e., last six years). It costs $17 million to purchase the mine. Furthermore, the operating cost in the first year is $1 million and is estimated to increase 10% each year. The price of the newly extracted gold is currently $50,000 per kg and is estimated to increase 5% annually (i.e., the price of the newly extracted gold in the first year of operations is estimated to be $52,500 per kg). Assume that the company uses an annual interest of 7% in their decision making.

Q32. Based on your solution from Question 13, the present value of this investment if the mine contains 500 kg of gold is $.

          

$1,690,203. Same as Question 13




Q33. The present value of this investment if the mine contains 300 kg of gold is $.

          

-$5,436,875. Same as Question 7




Q34. The present value of this investment if the mine contains 600 kg of gold is $.

          

$5,081,864. See Questions 7 and 13 for similar computations




Q35. Expected value describes the weighted average of the outcomes.



Based on your solutions from Questions 32 to 34, the expected present value of this investment is $.

          

0.25 x -$5,436,875 + 0.5 x $1,690,203 + 0.25 x $5,081,864 = $756,349




Q36. Variance and standard deviation (i.e., square root of variance) describe the dispersion of possible outcomes.



Based on your solutions from Questions 32 to 35, the standard deviation of the present value of this investment is $.

          

SQRT(0.25 x (-$5,436,875 - $756,349)2 + 0.5 x ($1,690,203 - $756,349)2 + 0.25 x ($5,081,864- $756,349)2) = $3,834,393




Option Valuation

A mining company is considering whether to invest in a newly discovered mine. The mine is expected to yield 100 kg of gold annually. There is a 0.25 probability the mine contains 300 kg of gold (i.e., last three years), 0.5 probability it contains 500 kg of gold (i.e., last five years) and 0.25 probability it contains 600 kg of gold (i.e., last six years). It costs $17 million to purchase the mine. Furthermore, the operating cost in the first year is $1 million and is estimated to increase 10% each year. The price of the newly extracted gold is currently $50,000 per kg and is estimated to increase 5% annually (i.e., the price of the newly extracted gold in the first year of operations is estimated to be $52,500 per kg). Assume that the company uses an annual interest of 7% in their decision making.

Suppose the mining company can perform a comprehensive analysis to determine the amount of gold the mine contains. The analysis costs $200,000. For simplicity, we assume that the results are available immediately. However, it is highly likely that the mine will no longer be available for purchase if the results are positive. Hence, the gold mine company will want to purchase an option to retain the right to purchase the gold mine for $17 million after the analysis is completed.

Q37. Suppose the mining company conducts the analysis and it reveals that the gold mine contains only 300 kg of gold. Hence, the company does not purchase the mine since it is unprofitable (see Question 33). Assuming that the option (i.e., right to purchase mine for $17 million) can be obtained for free, the company loses $ under this scenario.

          

Here, the company only loses the $200,000 it paid for the analysis




Q38. Suppose the mining company conducts the analysis and it reveals that the gold mine contains 500 kg of gold. Since it is profitable (see Question 32), the company will purchase the mine for $17 million. Assuming that the option (i.e., right to purchase mine for $17 million) is free, the present value of the investment (including the cost of analysis) is $ under this scenario.

          

$1,690,203 - $200,000 = $1,490,203




Q39. Suppose the mining company conducts the analysis and it reveals that the gold mine contains 600 kg of gold. Since it is profitable (see Question 34), the company will purchase the mine for $17 million. Assuming that the option (i.e., right to purchase mine for $17 million) is free, the present value of the investment (including the cost of analysis) is $ under this scenario.

          

$5,081,864 - $200,000 = $4,881,864




Q40. Suppose the mining company obtains the option for free, pays $200,000 for the analysis and purchases the mine for $17 million only if the analysis reveals that the gold mine contains at least 500 kg of gold. Based on your solutions from Questions 37 to 39, the expected present value of the above course of action is $.

          

0.25 x -$200,000 + 0.5 x $1,490,203 + 0.25 x $4,881,864 = $1,915,568




Q41. Based on your solutions from Questions 35 and 40, the option which allows the company to purchase the mine for $17 million after the analysis results are available is worth $.

          

$1,915,568 - $756,349 = $1,159,219




Q42. Suppose the mining company conducts the analysis (which costs $200,000) and purchases the option for $1,159,219.

Q42a. Under the scenario where the gold mine contains only 300 kg of gold, the company loses $.

          

$200,000 + $1,159,219 = $1,359,219




Q42b. Under the scenario where the gold mine contains 500 kg of gold, the present value of the investment (including the cost of analysis and option) is $.

          

$1,690,203 - $200,000 - $1,159,219 = $330,984




Q42c. Under the scenario where the gold mine contains 600 kg of gold, the present value of the investment (including the cost of analysis and option) is $.

          

$5,081,864 - $200,000 - $1,159,219 = $3,722,645




Q42d. The expected present value of the above course of action is $.

          

0.25 x -$1,359,219 + 0.5 x $330,984 + 0.25 x $3,722,645 = $756,349




Q42e. The standard deviation of the present value of the above course of action is $.

          

SQRT(0.25 x (-$1,359,219 - $756,349)2 + 0.5 x ($330,984 - $756,349)2 + 0.25 x ($3,722,645 - $756,349)2) = $1,846,375




Q43. Suppose the analysis costs $200,000 and the option costs $1,159,219. Consider the following two courses of actions:

a) Purchase the mine for $17 million immediately.

b) Purchase the option, conduct the analysis and purchase the mine only if the analysis reveals that the gold mine contains at least 500 kg of gold

Based on your solutions from Questions 35, 36 and 42, the first course of action (i.e., purchase immediately) is preferred.

          

False. The latter course of action is preferred since it has the same expected presented value but lower standard deviation (i.e., less dispersion and hence less risk).
