Single Cash Flow

An investment of $100 is made today at an annual interest rate of 2%.

Q1. What is the value of this investment one year from now?

$

After 1 year: $100 × (1 + 0.02)1 = $100 × 1.02 = $102

Q2. What is the value of this investment two years from now?

$

After 2 years: $100 × (1.02)2 = $100 × 1.0404 = $104.04

Q3. What is the value of this investment three years from now?

$

After 3 years: $100 × (1.02)3 = $100 × 1.0612 = $106.12

Q4. Suppose instead the investment is made for 20 years at an annual interest rate of 2%. What is the value of this investment 20 years from now? Round to the nearest dollar.

$

$100 × (1.02)20 = $100 × 1.4859... ≈ $149 (accept $148–$149)

Q5. Suppose instead the investment is $50,000 made for 20 years at an annual interest rate of 2.5%. What is the value of this investment 20 years from now? Round to the nearest dollar.

$

$50,000 × (1.025)20 = $50,000 × 1.6386... ≈ $82,034

Uniform Cash Flow

An investment of $100 is made each year for three years. The annual interest rate is 2%.

Q6. What is the value of the first $100 payment immediately after it is made (at the end of year 1)?

$

The first payment has just been made, so its value right now is exactly $100.

Q7. What is the value of the first $100 payment one year after it is made (at the end of year 2)?

$

$100 × (1.02)1 = $102

Q8. At the end of year 2, the second $100 payment is also made. What is the combined value of both the first payment (which has now grown for 1 year) and the second payment at the end of year 2?

$

First payment grown: $102 + second payment: $100 = $202

Q9. What is the value of this combined sum one year later (at the end of year 3)?

$

$202 × (1.02)1 = $206.04

Q10. At the end of year 3, the third $100 payment is made. What is the combined value of all three payments at the end of year 3?

$

$206.04 + $100 = $306.04

Q11. Using the formula for the future value of a uniform series:
FV = A × (1+i)N − 1/i
where A = $100, i = 2%, N = 25 years, what is the future value? Round to the nearest dollar.

$

FV = 100 × ((1.02)25 − 1) / 0.02 = 100 × (1.6406 − 1) / 0.02 = 100 × 32.03 = $2,429

Q12. Using the same formula with A = $200, i = 2%, N = 30 years, what is the future value? Round to the nearest dollar.

$

FV = 200 × ((1.02)30 − 1) / 0.02 = 200 × 40.568 = $9,132

Loan Payments

A borrower makes five equal annual payments of $100 at an annual interest rate of 2%.

Q13. Using the formula provided in Question 11, these five payments are equivalent to a single payment of $_____ five years from now.

$

FV = 100 × ((1.02)5 − 1) / 0.02 = 100 × 5.2040 = $520.40 — but with the values from Q11 context (A=$20,000, i=4%, N=5): FV = 20000 × ((1.04)5−1)/0.04 = $110,408

Q14. Using the formula provided in Question 5 and your solution to Question 13, these five payments are equivalent to a single payment of $_____ immediately.

$

PV = FV / (1+i)N = 110,408 / (1.04)5 = $100,000

Q15. It follows from combining the formulas in Q5 and Q11 that the present value formula for a uniform series is:
PV = A × (1+i)N − 1/i × (1+i)N
Using this formula with A = $20,000, i = 4%, N = 5, verify your answer to Q14 and compute the present value.

$

PV = 5000 × ((1.03)5−1)/(0.03×(1.03)5) = $22,462

Increasing Cash Flows

Increasing (or growing) cash flows follow a geometric series where each payment grows by a fixed percentage g per period. The present value of a growing annuity is:

PV = A × 1 − ((1+g)/(1+i))N/i − g

where A is the first payment, i is the discount rate, g is the growth rate, and N is the number of periods.

Yes — all three approaches are mathematically equivalent. Each one correctly discounts the four $2,000 payments back to time 0, so they all yield $37,269. The answer is True.
