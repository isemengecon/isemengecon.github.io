Economic Life

1$22,500$3,000

Here, we assume an annual interest rate of 10% in our computations.

Q1. Suppose the forklift truck is replaced each year. The cash flow under this replacement policy is illustrated by the cash flow on the left. This cash flow is equivalent to a single cost payment of $ at the end of the first year. This cost is commonly referred to as the equivalent uniform annual cost (EUAC). In particular, the EUAC can be interpreted as the annual cost of owning and operating this forklift truck under a given replacement policy.

          

$30,000 × 1.1 − $22,500 + $3,000 = $13,500




Q2. The EUAC of owning and operating this forklift truck when it is replaced once every two years is $.

          

EUAC = ($30,000 + $3,000⁄1.1 + $4,500 − $16,875⁄1.12) × (A/P,10%,2)

= $22,500 × 0.5762

= $12,965




Q3. The EUAC of owning and operating this forklift truck when it is replaced once every three years is $.

          

EUAC = ($30,000 + $3,000⁄1.1 + $4,500⁄1.12 + $7,000 − $12,750⁄1.13)(A/P,10%,3)

= $32,126.22 × 0.4021

= $12,918




Q4. The EUAC of owning and operating this forklift truck when it is replaced once every four years is $.

          

EUAC = ($30,000 + $3,000⁄1.1 + $4,500⁄1.12 + $7,000⁄1.13 + $10,000 − $9,750⁄1.14)(A/P,10%,4)

= $41,876.24 × 0.3155

= $13,212




Q5. The EUAC of owning and operating this forklift truck when it is replaced once every five years is $.

          

EUAC = ($30,000 + $3,000⁄1.1 + $4,500⁄1.12 + $7,000⁄1.13 + $10,000⁄1.14 + $13,000 − $7,125⁄1.15)(A/P,10%,5)

= $52,183.53 × 0.2638

= $13,766

Q30. What is the advantage of determining economic life based on the marginal cost (approach used in this section) rather than actual cash flows (approach used in the previous section)?

Based on marginal total cost, we can infer if increasing the service life of the equipment will increase or decrease EUAC. Hence, there is no need to compute the actual EUAC value when increasing the service life of the equipment clearly increases EUAC. This is helpful since computing marginal total cost is relatively easier than computing EUAC.

After-Tax Costs

In this section, we examine the effects of tax on costs. Suppose that your annual revenue is $100M and assume a flat tax rate of 20%.

Q38. If you earn this $100M without incurring any costs:

Q38a. Before-tax profit (in $M) is $M.

          

Since there are no costs, then profit = revenue




Q38b. Tax payable (in $M) is $M.

          

Tax = $100M × 0.2 = $20M




Q38c. After-tax profit (in $M) is $M.

          

After-tax profit = $100M − $20M = $80M




Q39. Suppose you had to run a $30M marketing campaign to generate the $100M in revenue. If there were no further costs:

Q39a. Before-tax profit (in $M) is $M.

          

Before-tax profit = $100M − $30M = $70M




Q39b. Tax payable (in $M) is $M.

          

Tax = $70M × 0.2 = $14M




Q39c. After-tax profit (in $M) is $M.

          

After-tax profit = $70M − $14M = $56M




Q40. Based on your solutions from Questions 38c and 39c, the $30M marketing has decreased after-tax profits (in $M) by $M.

          

$80M − $56M = $24M




Q41. Based on your solutions from Questions 38b and 39b, the $30M marketing has decreased the tax payable (in $M) by $M.

          

$20M − $14M = $6M




Q42. Based on your solutions from Questions 40 and 41, we observe that after-tax cost is lower than before-tax cost due to tax savings.

          

Although marketing cost is $30M, there was a corresponding $6M tax savings as a result of this expense. Hence, the marketing cost after-tax is just $24M instead of $30M.




After-Tax EUAC

In the first section, we looked at how to determine the annual cost of owning and operating equipment. In the previous section, we highlighted that costs are lower after tax considerations. In the context of owning and operating equipment, annual expenses and depreciation both reduce the amount of tax payable. In this section, we examine how after-tax EUAC can be computed.

Suppose a new forklift truck costs $30,000. Its subsequent market values (i.e., amount that equipment can sell for) and annual expenses are listed below:

End of Year, kMarket Value at End of Year kAnnual Expense in Year k
0$30,000N.A.
1$22,500$3,000
2$16,875$4,500
3$12,750$7,000
4$9,750$10,000
5$7,125$13,000


Assume a flat tax rate of 20%, SL depreciation across 3 years with zero salvage value and an annual interest rate of 8%.

Q43. Suppose the forklift truck is sold off at the end of the first year:

Q43a. The depreciation in the first year is $.

          

$30,000 ÷ 3 = $10,000




Q43b. Book value at the end of the first year is $.

          

$30,000 − $10,000 = $20,000




Q43c. Capital gain from sale of forklift truck is $.

          

$22,500 − $20,000 = $2,500




Q43d. Tax savings from owning and operating forklift truck for one year is $.

          

($3,000 + $10,000 − $2,500) × 0.2 = $2,100




Q43e. The after-tax cash flow (ATCF) in Year 1 is $.

          

$22,500 − $3,000 + $2,100 = $21,600




Q43f. Based on the diagram below, after-tax EUAC of owning and operating this forklift truck for one year is $:

          

$30,000 × 1.08 − $21,600 = $10,800




Q44. Suppose the forklift truck is sold off at the end of the second year:

Q44a. The depreciation in the first year is $.

          

$30,000 ÷ 3 = $10,000




Q44b. Book value at the end of the first year is $.

          

$30,000 − $10,000 = $20,000




Q44c. Tax savings from owning and operating forklift truck in the first year is $.

Q44d. The after-tax cash flow (ATCF) in Year 1 is $.

          

−$3,000 + $2,600 = −$400




Q44e. The depreciation in the second year is $.

          

$20,000 ÷ 2 = $10,000

$20,000 − $10,000 = $10,000




Q44g. Capital gain from sale of forklift truck is $.

          

$16,875 − $10,000 = $6,875




Q44h. Tax savings from owning and operating forklift truck in the second year is $.

Q44i. The after-tax cash flow (ATCF) in Year 2 is $.

          

$16,875 − $4,500 + $1,525 = $13,900

after-tax EUAC = ($30,000 + $400⁄1.08 − $13,900⁄1.082) × (A/P,8%,2)

= $18,453.36 × 0.5608
